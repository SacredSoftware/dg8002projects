Displacement mapping is a computer graphics technique that uses a texture or heightmap to alter the geometric position of points over the surface of an object. This 'displacement' of the object's surface creates an additional sense of depth and detail, allowng for self-occlusion, self-shadowing and silhouettes. The specfics of the surface gemoetry are calculated through a texture function, which uses the pixel values of the provided texture map, as well as any additional parameters to map geometrical changes in 3d space. While powerful, displacement mapping is the most costly type of mapping technique due to the large amount of additional geometry that must be cacluated.

How Displacement Mapping Works

Step 1:
Generate Texture

Using an image editing software, create a pattern where each individual pixel is given a greyscale RGB value ranging from black to white. The pattern can be randomized or calibrated using an exisitng 'heightmap' that converts height data into pixel values.

Step 2:
Apply Texture to Object

In your prefered 3d modelling software, create a 3d object and corresponding 'material' that dictates how the surface of the object appears. Under the material parameters, assign your generated texture as the material's 'displacement map/heightmap'.

Step 3:
Create Displacement

Adjust your material's displacement/heightmap parameters to transform your object's surface geometry. Your transformatons will scale based on the colour of your texture - where the more white a pixel is, the more that section of the surface will be displaced.
